## Why one row of this table has more blanks than the others

The three shafts on this page are not published the same way, and that difference is worth more to a buyer than any single number.

The **Victory VAP Target Sport V6** publishes a complete chart on its own listing: every spine size from 400 to 1200, with the grains per inch for each one, plus a diameter and a straightness tolerance. A buyer can choose a spine from the page alone.

The **Easton Avance Sport 4mm** publishes its spine range, 340 to 2000 across 16 sizes, and calls itself a "4mm" shaft by name. It does not publish a grains-per-inch figure on this listing. A buyer picks a spine number but has to find arrow mass from elsewhere, or weigh built arrows once they arrive.

The **Easton X10** publishes a spine range, 325 to 1000, and stops there. It also has no single diameter to publish: the shaft is barrelled, wider at the centre than at the ends, by design. Points, nocks and pins are sold separately, and matching a spine to a bow is normally done with a coach or pro shop rather than read off a chart.

None of this means the Easton shafts are worse. It means they are sold to two different kinds of buyer.

## What that difference actually costs

**Price.** VAP is $124.99. Avance Sport is $109.99, $15 less. X10 is $499.99, four times the VAP.

**Spine range.** VAP runs 400 to 1200 in ten sizes. Avance Sport runs 340 to 2000 in 16 sizes, the widest of the three. X10 runs 325 to 1000 in 14 sizes.

**Pack size.** All three are sold as a dozen shafts.

A dozen X10 shafts costs roughly what four dozen VAP shafts would. That gap is the real decision on this page, and it is not explained by any single published spec.

## Why a barreled shaft has no one diameter

The X10 not publishing a diameter is not a gap in Lancaster's listing. It is a property of the shaft.

A parallel shaft, like the VAP or the Avance Sport, is the same outer diameter along its whole length, so one number describes it. A barrelled shaft is thicker in the centre and tapers toward each end, which Easton markets as improving clearance past the arrow rest and forgiveness on release. One diameter figure would misdescribe a barrelled shaft rather than simplify it.

If a page elsewhere quotes a single diameter for the X10, ask which point on the shaft it was measured at.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the Victory VAP Target Sport V6 if you are choosing your own spine and want every number in front of you before you order. It is the only shaft here you can select correctly without outside help.

Buy the Easton Avance Sport 4mm if price matters more than a published weight chart, and you are willing to find grains per inch elsewhere or weigh the finished arrow.

Buy the Easton X10 only once a coach or pro shop has already matched a spine to your bow and draw. It is not the shaft to select from a chart on a first purchase.
