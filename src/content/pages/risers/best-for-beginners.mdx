## How this page differs from the under $500 page

Two riser pages on this site answer two different questions.

The [under $500 comparison](/risers/under-500/) answers "what can I get for my money", and stops at a price cap.

This page answers "what should a first barebow riser actually have", and covers every price from $119.99 to $859.99, because the answer to that question does not stop at $500. Two of the seven risers here cost more than the cap and are included so the feature question can be answered honestly.

## The three things that matter, and why

**ILF fitting.** The limb attachment standard. It matters because it is how you change poundage later: new limbs, same riser. All seven risers here are ILF, so this one is easy.

**A plunger mounting.** A cushion plunger is legal in barebow and is standard equipment, under [World Archery article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3). The riser needs a Berger hole to take one.

**Somewhere to bolt weights.** Barebow bans stabilisers, so weights are how a barebow archer manages balance. The 2022 bylaw permits weights above and below the grip, and the whole bow must still pass the 12.2 cm ring under article 9.3.6.

Length is not on that list. All seven risers here are 25 inches, which is the standard full-length riser and the right choice for a beginner.

## Only four of seven publish a plunger mounting

This is the finding worth having, and it is the same problem the [tabs comparison](/tabs/best-stringwalking-tabs/) turned up.

Four listings state a plunger or Berger hole mounting outright: the Galaxy Crescent, the Kinetic Vygo, the Sebastien Flute EVO and the WNS Quantum AX.

Three do not: the Hoyt RCRV Podium, and both Gillos, which are the two most expensive risers on this page at $829.99 and $859.99.

That does not mean those three cannot take a plunger. Gillo builds barebow risers and it would be surprising if they could not. It means their Lancaster listings do not say so, and this site reports what is published rather than what is assumed. If a plunger mounting is the reason you are buying, confirm it with the maker before ordering.

## Only one publishes an included weight system

The Kinetic Vygo ships with two 200 g weights and one 100 g weight, mounting below the grip. Nothing else on this page includes weights.

The two Gillos publish weight mounting without weights: the GT has "special round seats for optional balancing weight" compatible with the G1, GQ and G5 accessory range, and the G1-N has bottom round seats for Gillo disk weights and the UFO V-Bar system. Both are designed around a weight ecosystem you buy into separately, and the [weights comparison](/weights/) shows what that costs: the Gillo G4 Hammers kit alone is $264.99.

The WNS Quantum AX publishes upper and lower 5/16 x 24 mounts on the back of the riser, which is the standard thread that the universal weights on the weights page use.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the Kinetic Vygo at $259.99 if you want a riser built for barebow that arrives ready to shoot it. It is the only riser here that publishes a plunger mounting and includes weights, which is the combination a beginner actually needs.

Buy the Galaxy Crescent at $119.99 if the budget is tight. It publishes a plunger mounting, it is ILF, and it costs less than half the Vygo. It supplies no weights, so budget for those separately.

Buy the Gillo GT at $829.99 only if you already know you are staying in the sport and want to buy into the Gillo weight system. It is the heaviest riser here at a published 1350 g, which a barebow archer generally wants, but it is over six times the price of the Crescent and its listing does not state a plunger mounting.

Do not buy on price alone in either direction. The most expensive risers here are not the ones that publish the most barebow-specific features.

## Where these numbers came from

Every specification and price is as published by [Lancaster Archery Supply](#source-lancaster-risers), read on 13 and 14 August 2026. Prices are one retailer's, on one day, in one country.

No riser on this page has been handled, weighed or shot by this site. The 12.2 cm ring column is blank for every row, for the reason set out on the [under $500 page](/risers/under-500/): the ring test applies to a complete bow with its attachments, so no riser can pass or fail it on its own.
