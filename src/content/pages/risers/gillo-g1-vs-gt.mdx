## Why this page is not really G1 vs GT

The title asks for the Gillo G1 against the Gillo GT. The honest page is different, and it is worth explaining why before the numbers.

The original Gillo G1 25" is listed by Lancaster Archery Supply as "NO LONGER AVAILABLE". It carries no current price. A comparison that put a live price next to a discontinued one would mislead a reader who is actually about to spend money.

Gillo's own successor to the G1 is the **Gillo G1-N**, in stock and built for the same purpose: a barebow-focused riser with Gillo's weight and grip system. This page compares the G1-N against the GT, which is the real choice a buyer faces today.

The G1's own specification is worth keeping on record, since it explains where the G1-N came from. Lancaster's archived listing gives it as CNC 6061 T6 aluminium, 25" or 27", 2.95 lb (1340 g), with recessed weight pockets under the handle for barebow balancing plates.

## What actually separates the G1-N and the GT

Both are current, both are ILF, and both are marketed to barebow archers. The differences are smaller than the page title suggests.

**Price.** The G1-N is $859.99. The GT is $829.99, $30 less.

**Mass weight.** The G1-N is 1370 g. The GT is 1350 g, 20 g less. Neither figure has been measured here.

**Purpose.** The G1-N is built for barebow and recurve together, with 43 mm deflex geometry described as giving "controlled flex and precision". The GT is marketed the same way, for "Leading Pro Performance Recurve and Barebow Technology", with integrated shock absorbers the G1-N's listing does not mention.

**Weight mounting.** The G1-N uses bottom round seats compatible with Gillo disk weights and the Gillo UFO V-Bar system. The GT uses its own round seats, and is listed as compatible with G1, GQ and G5 weights and accessories, which suggests broader compatibility across Gillo's own product line.

## Neither riser publishes a barebow-specific ring result

Both risers are sold with barebow buyers in mind, and neither maker publishes a result for the 12.2 cm ring test.

That is expected, not a gap in Lancaster's listing. The ring test applies to the complete bow with weights and dampeners fitted, under the [2022 World Archery bylaw](#source-wa-dampener-bylaw), not to a bare riser. No riser on its own can pass or fail it.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the Gillo G1-N if you want a riser designed for barebow specifically and you plan to use Gillo's own weight system. It costs $30 more than the GT and is the newer of the two designs.

Buy the Gillo GT if $30 matters, or if you want a riser that is equally at home in Olympic recurve should you ever switch divisions. Its integrated shock absorbers are also a published feature the G1-N's listing does not carry.

Do not buy either expecting the original G1. If a listing for the G1 25" Matte shows a price, check the date. This page found it discontinued on 13 August 2026.

## Where these numbers came from

Every specification and price is as published by [Lancaster Archery Supply](#source-lancaster-gillo), read on 13 August 2026. Prices are one retailer's, on one day, in one country, and Gillo's risers move less often than budget risers but they do move.
