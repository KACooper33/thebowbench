## Why the ring column is empty

The comparison table above carries a column for the 12.2 cm ring, and every row in it is blank. That is deliberate.

[World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.6](#source-wa-book-3) applies the ring test to the whole bow, "including any combination of weights, vibration dampeners, and other attachments". A riser on its own cannot pass or fail it. The answer depends on which weights you fit and how far they stand off the riser.

No maker publishes a ring result, because no maker can. Any site that prints one for a bare riser has invented it. The column stays on this page because the template fixes the columns, and a blank cell is more honest than a guess.

## Why a barebow riser is not just a recurve riser

Every riser on this page is sold as an ILF recurve riser, and four of the five are not marketed for barebow at all. That matters less than it sounds, and more than it sounds, for one reason each way.

It matters less because barebow equipment is recurve equipment minus the sight and the stabilisers, under [World Archery article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3). The riser itself does not change.

It matters more because a barebow archer replaces stabiliser mass with weights bolted to the riser. The [2022 World Archery bylaw](#source-wa-dampener-bylaw) permits weights "below and above the riser's grip". A riser with mounting points in both places gives more tuning range than one with bushings only on the back.

That single feature separates the five risers below far more than price does.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the Kinetic Vygo 25" Barebow if you want to shoot barebow without a second purchase. It is the only riser on this page that ships with weights, and it publishes mounting points both on the back and on the face below the grip.

Buy the Galaxy Crescent 25" if the budget is the constraint. It costs $119.99, accepts a plunger, and leaves room to buy weights later.

Buy the WNS Quantum AX 25" if you want published mounts above and below the grip and intend to choose your own weights.

Buy the Hoyt RCRV Podium 25" if you want the best known name under the cap and already own a weight system.

Do not buy on mass weight alone. The published figures here span 2.01 lb to 1240 g, which is a real difference, but a barebow archer adds far more than that in weights.

## What $500 does not buy

The cap in this page's title excludes two risers worth knowing about.

The Gillo GX2 25" ILF Barebow is $519.99, which misses the cap by $19.99. It is a barebow riser by design rather than an ILF riser used for barebow.

The Gillo GT 25" is $829.99, and publishes a mass weight of 1350 g, heavier than anything here. Both prices are Lancaster's on 12 August 2026.

If you can stretch, look at the first of those before you settle. If you cannot, nothing on this page will hold you back for your first two seasons.

## Where these numbers came from

Every specification on this page is the maker's published figure as listed by [Lancaster Archery Supply](#source-lancaster-ilf-risers) on 12 August 2026. Nothing was weighed here, nothing was shot, and no riser on this page has been handled.

Prices are one retailer's, on one day, in one country. They move, and two of the risers listed above were already discounted from a higher published price when this page was written.

When measured weights for any of these risers exist on this site, the table will say measured instead of maker, and this section will say what tool was used.
