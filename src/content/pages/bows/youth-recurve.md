## The only question that matters is whether the bow grows

A young archer gets stronger. The bow does not.

That single fact separates the six bows on this page more than price does, and no retail listing frames it as a decision.

**A takedown bow grows.** The riser stays and you buy heavier limbs. The Galaxy Bullseye 54" publishes limbs at 10, 15, 20 and 25 lb. A child starting at 10 lb can move up three times without a new bow.

**A one-piece bow does not.** The Arc Rolan Snake 50" is 16 lb at 24 inches, permanently. When it is outgrown it is finished, and the maker publishes a 26 inch draw length ceiling on top of that.

At $54.99 against $129.98 the one-piece looks like the saving. Buy two of them across three years and it is not.

## What the takedown column does not tell you

Takedown means the limbs come off. It does not mean any limbs fit.

Every takedown bow on this page uses its own maker's fitting, not ILF, the interchange standard used by target risers and limbs sold as separate parts. So heavier limbs have to come from the same maker as the riser.

That matters most for the PSE Razorback Jr. PSE's own store does not list this bow. The product page redirects to their homepage and their site search returns nothing for it, checked on 14 August 2026. Retailers still stock it, and the Sportsman's Warehouse listing showed it out of stock with shipping unavailable on that date.

A bow whose limbs must come from one maker, and whose maker no longer lists it, is a narrower upgrade path than the takedown column suggests.

## Two bows take a cushion plunger, which is unusual at this price

A cushion plunger is the one piece of barebow tuning equipment the rules permit. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.3](#source-wa-book-3) allows "an adjustable pressure button, pressure point or arrow plate". Most youth bows ship with a stick-on rest and no way to fit one.

Two here publish a thread for it.

The **Galaxy Little Fox** lists "plunger, stabilizer, and sight/quiver bushings". The **PSE Razorback Jr.** is published as "Threaded for Cushion Plunger" with stabiliser and sight bushings.

The other four state stabiliser and sight bushings, or state nothing. That is reported as not stated rather than as absent, because a listing that omits a feature is not a listing that denies it.

This is not a small detail if a young archer sticks with the sport. It is the difference between a bow that can be tuned and one that cannot.

## A published figure on this page is wrong

The Galaxy Bullseye 48" listing states a recommended brace height of 14½ inches.

That is not a plausible brace height for a 48 inch recurve. The 54 inch model from the same maker, on the same retailer, states 6½ to 7½ inches. Recurve brace heights sit in that range.

The figure has been left out of the table rather than repeated. If you buy that bow, set brace height from the string that comes with it and ignore the listing.

This is the kind of error that spreads. A specification gets copied from a retail listing to a review site to a forum post, and nobody checks whether it is physically possible.

## Which one should you buy?

**Buy the Galaxy Bullseye 54"** if you want one bow to last. Four published draw weights on one riser is the widest upgrade range here, and 54 inches suits a child who is still growing into it. It does not publish a plunger thread, which is the trade.

**Buy the Galaxy Little Fox** if you want the plunger thread and three length options, and the extra $19.99 over the Bullseye is not the deciding factor. It stops at 20 lb where the Bullseye reaches 25 lb.

**Buy the Arc Rolan Snake 50"** if this is a trial rather than a commitment. It is the cheapest way to find out whether a child takes to archery, and it is honest about being a recreation bow.

**Be careful with the PSE Razorback Jr.** It is a good specification at $119.99 and it takes a plunger. It is also the hardest of the six to buy, and its maker no longer lists it.

**Skip the Bear Marksman** unless 23 lb is already known to suit the archer. One draw weight, right hand only, and no stated takedown.

Beginner sets, the $15 to $60 bow-and-two-arrows kits, are a different purchase and are not compared here. They are toys that shoot, and they are fine at that, but nothing about them grows with a child.

## The first weight measured on this site

No maker on this page publishes a mass weight. Not one of the six listings states how heavy the bow is, which is the sort of gap this site exists to fill.

The PSE Razorback Jr. was weighed here on 14 August 2026, on a kitchen scale reading ounces.

| Condition | Weight |
|---|---|
| Unstrung, aftermarket rest fitted, plunger removed | **1 lb 12.16 oz, 798 g** |
| Strung, otherwise the same | 1 lb 12.4 oz, 805 g |

The 6.8 g difference between them is the bowstring, which is about right for the Dacron string supplied. The unstrung figure is the one in the table, because that is the convention makers use when they publish a mass weight.

Two things that number is not. It is not the bow as it left the factory, because the stock rest has been replaced. And it is not a claim about the other five bows, whose weights remain unknown.

### Tiller, and a brace height that proves less than it looks

Three more measurements were taken the same day, with a tape measure, braced, perpendicular to the string.

| From the string to | Reading |
|---|---|
| Upper limb butt | 7 1/8 in |
| Lower limb butt | 7 1/16 in |
| Grip pivot point | 7 1/2 in |

**Tiller is +1/16 in**, upper minus lower. That is a small positive tiller, and on a bow at this price it says the two limbs are reasonably well matched. No maker at this end of the market publishes tiller, and no review of a youth bow reports it.

**The brace height reads 7 1/2 in against a published 6 1/2 in**, and that difference is worth an inch of caution rather than a headline.

The string is the original, but it has been taken off and refitted several times. Twists set brace height, and a string that has been restrung an unknown number of times does not carry the factory twist count. So this figure describes this bow today. It is not evidence that the published figure is wrong.

The direction is worth noting too. Dacron stretches with use, which lowers brace height over time. This one reads high, which points at twists picked up during restringing rather than at anything about the product.

Tiller survives that problem, because it is a difference between two limbs measured in one session at one brace height. That is why it is reported as a finding and the brace height is not.

For scale, 798 g for a complete 54 inch bow with limbs is lighter than a bare Galaxy Crescent 25" target riser at a published 912 g, with no limbs on it at all. A youth bow has to be light enough for a child to hold up, and this is what that looks like.
