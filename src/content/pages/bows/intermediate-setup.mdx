## Why the middle setup wins

The Kinetic Vygo costs $140 more than the Galaxy Crescent. That looks like a lot on a first bow, until you price what it includes.

The Vygo ships with a barebow weight system: two 200 g weights and one 100 g weight, 500 g in total, mounting below the grip. The cheapest single weight on the [weights comparison page](/weights/) is a 100 g Mybo at $19.99. Buying 500 g separately at that rate is about $100.

So the real premium over the cheapest riser is closer to $40 than $140, and you get a riser designed around barebow weight mounting rather than a recurve riser you are adapting.

Barebow replaces stabiliser mass with weights, because [World Archery article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3) bans stabilisers outright. A riser that ships ready for that is doing something a cheaper riser is not.

## What is deliberately missing from every setup

**Weights, in the two cheaper setups.** The Vygo includes them. The other two do not, and that is fine to start. Weights tune balance, and there is nothing to tune until you are shooting consistently.

**A sight, a clicker and stabilisers.** All three are banned in barebow. The [barebow rules guide](/guides/barebow-rules/) covers each with its article number, and the [start here page](/start-here/) covers what else can wait.

**A bow package.** Packages were searched for and none published its contents clearly enough to price honestly against these parts. When one does, it will appear here with its contents listed.

## Is a built setup actually an upgrade over a complete bow?

This is the question this page exists to answer, and the answer is narrower than the price difference suggests.

A complete [beginner takedown recurve](/bows/takedown-recurve/) starts at $99.95. The Galaxy Sage at $149.98 is the pick on that page, and it publishes a plunger bushing, which matters here because the setups below all include a plunger. Add the same tab, plunger and arrows used above, $19.99, $39.99 and $109.99, and the Sage reaches a shooting setup for **$319.95**. That is $70 below the cheapest setup on this page.

The Black Hunter ILF at $159.95 is the only ILF bow on that page, but its listing describes no bushings, so a plunger cannot be assumed to fit it. That is why the comparison above uses the Sage.

So what does the extra money buy?

**Not upgradeability.** The Black Hunter ILF is ILF, and so is the Galaxy Crescent build. Both take any ILF limb from any maker. On the one thing that decides whether a first bow lasts, the cheap complete bow is equal.

**Weights, and a narrower fittings gap than this page used to claim.** Barebow bans stabilisers, so weights are how the bow is balanced.

Until 25 August 2026 this section said no complete takedown bow publishes weight mounting at all. That was wrong, and it is corrected here rather than quietly deleted. **Five of the eight bows on the [beginner takedown page](/bows/takedown-recurve/) publish a stabiliser bushing**, which is a thread a weight bolts into.

Two real differences survive that correction. **None of those eight ships a weight**, where the Kinetic Vygo includes 500 g in the box. And none of them publishes mounting both above and below the grip, which is the arrangement the [2022 World Archery bylaw](/risers/under-500/) permits and the one that lets a barebow archer move balance in both directions rather than one.

**The gap is not uniform across this page either.** The Kinetic Vygo publishes mounting below the grip and includes the weights. The WNS Quantum AX publishes upper and lower mounts on the back of the riser. The Galaxy Crescent, which is the riser in the $389.95 build, publishes "stabiliser bushings only" — the same answer a $149.99 complete bow gives. On weight mounting alone, the cheapest setup on this page is not ahead of the cheaper route.

The Black Hunter ILF does not state a plunger bushing, where four of the risers here do.

**A riser chosen on its own merits.** Machined aluminium rather than laminated wood, a known mass weight, and a choice of limb independent of the riser.

### Which route is right

| You want | Route |
|---|---|
| The cheapest bow that can grow | Complete takedown bow, $159.95 |
| To shoot barebow properly, with weights | A setup from this page, from $389.95 |
| To choose riser and limbs separately | A setup from this page |
| To find out whether you like archery | Complete takedown bow, $99.95 |

The progression that makes sense is a complete bow first, then this page when you know you are staying and want the fittings barebow actually needs. Buying an intermediate setup as a first purchase is not wrong, it is just more than the sport asks of you on day one.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the barebow-ready setup at $529.95 if you can. The riser is built for the division you are entering, and the included weights make the price difference much smaller than it looks.

Buy the cheapest complete setup at $389.95 if $530 is not available. It is a complete, legal barebow setup, and no part of it will hold back a beginner. Add weights later.

Buy the room-to-grow setup at $869.95 only if you already know this is your sport. Every part of it is a real upgrade, but none of them will make a new archer shoot better, and the [plunger comparison](/plungers/beiter-vs-shibuya/) explains why the expensive plunger buys repeatability rather than accuracy.
