## Why a marked tab is barebow equipment, not an accessory

Marks on a tab are legal in barebow because the rulebook says so directly. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.7](#source-wa-book-3) permits marks or lines on the tab face, provided they "must be uniform in size, shape and colour and may have up to two different lengths". The [barebow rules guide](/guides/barebow-rules/) covers this in full, alongside string walking itself, which article 9.3.5 permits outright.

That is why a stringwalking tab is not an upgrade over a plain tab. It is the standard tool for one of the two aiming methods barebow allows.

## One listing does not state the feature this page is about

The Yost Archery 3-Under Pro Tab is a recognised name in US barebow archery, built from CNC machined aluminium with a cordovan face. Its Lancaster listing describes "a machine etched index, accents, and logo" and stops there. It does not use the words stringwalking or crawl mark anywhere in the published copy.

The Spigarelli and the Avalon both state the feature plainly. Spigarelli: "etched markings for precise string walking marks." Avalon: "distinctive laser engraved markings ... for precise crawl measurements."

This does not mean the Yost tab lacks a usable index. It means this specific listing makes no claim about it, and this page reports what is published rather than what a well-known brand is assumed to include.

## What the three actually differ on

**Price.** Avalon $19.99, Spigarelli $44.99, Yost $51.05. The gap between the cheapest and the most expensive is $31.06, more than the Avalon costs outright.

**Hand sizing.** The Avalon publishes five sizes, X-small through X-large, in separate RH and LH variants. The Yost publishes two sizes, small and large, also separate RH and LH. The Spigarelli publishes one size, medium, and is ambidextrous, one listing for both hands.

**Plate material.** Yost states CNC machined 6000 series aluminium. Spigarelli states an anodised aluminium plate assembled with stainless screws. Avalon states a grey anodised aluminium frame. All three use aluminium; none publishes a thickness or a weight.

No maker on this page publishes tab weight. A comparison that ranks these on "feel" or "how light it is" without a published or measured number is repeating an impression, not reporting a fact.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the Avalon Classic Barebow Tab if this is your first stringwalking tab. It is the cheapest tab here, it states its crawl marks plainly, and five published sizes make it the easiest of the three to fit to a beginner's hand.

Buy the Spigarelli Spiga Tab BB+ if you want a machined plate and ambidextrous sizing removes any doubt about fit. It costs more than the Avalon and less than the Yost, and states the one feature this page is about.

Be cautious with the Yost 3-Under Pro Tab if published stringwalking marks specifically are the reason you are buying. Confirm with the maker or a product photo before ordering, since the listing used here does not claim them.
