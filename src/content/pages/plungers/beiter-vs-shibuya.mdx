## Both plungers are legal in barebow

Neither plunger will fail equipment inspection in a barebow event. A cushion plunger is a tuning device, not an aiming aid.

[World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.3](#source-wa-book-3) permits "an adjustable pressure button, pressure point or arrow plate ... provided they do not offer any additional aid in aiming".

One limit applies to both, and it is about where the plunger sits rather than which one you buy. The same article places the pressure point "no further back than 2 cm towards the athlete from the pivot point of the grip". The [barebow rules guide](/guides/barebow-rules/) covers that measurement in full.

## What the five times price gap actually buys

The Beiter Plunger is $199.99. The Shibuya DX Plunger is $39.99. Both prices are Lancaster Archery Supply's on 12 August 2026.

Both ship three springs. Both use a stainless steel cylinder. Both include the hex tooling to fit and adjust them. On the things a plunger must do, the published specifications are closer than the price gap suggests.

Two published differences separate them.

**The Beiter Plunger publishes its spring tensions.** They are listed as .045, .060 and .070. Shibuya publishes only that three springs of different strengths are included.

**The Beiter Plunger publishes micro-click adjustment.** Shibuya publishes no adjustment mechanism for the DX at all.

Together those mean one thing: with the Beiter you can write down a setting and return to it. With the Shibuya DX you cannot, at least not from any number the maker supplies.

## What neither maker publishes

Neither maker states how far one click or one turn moves the tip, so neither can be compared on adjustment resolution from published figures alone. The Beiter's micro-click is described without a click count.

This site has not measured either plunger. When it does, the table on this page will say measured instead of maker, and the method section will say what tool was used.

Be careful with plunger figures found elsewhere. Spring tension and tip travel are exactly the numbers most often repeated between sites without anyone measuring them.

## Which one should you buy?

Buy the Shibuya DX Plunger if this is your first plunger. It is $39.99, it is legal in barebow, and it does the job a plunger exists to do. A beginner will gain more from arrows that match their bow than from a plunger five times the price.

Buy the Beiter Plunger if you tune often and need to return to an exact previous setting. That is the capability the price buys, and it is worth it only to an archer who already tunes deliberately.

Buy neither yet if your arrows are not matched to your setup. A plunger corrects a small amount of arrow behaviour. It does not fix badly spined arrows, and no plunger at any price will.

## A note on the Shibuya DX tips

The Shibuya DX Plunger ships with two tips, one teflon and one metal. That is a published feature and it is unusual at the price.

It also means the DX has a consumable part. A tip that wears changes plunger behaviour gradually, which is harder to notice than a part that fails outright. If you buy the DX, keep the spare tip.

## Where these numbers came from

Every specification and every price on this page is as published by [Lancaster Archery Supply](#source-lancaster-plungers), read on 12 August 2026.

Prices are one retailer's, on one day, in one country. Plunger prices move less than riser prices, but they do move.

No plunger on this page has been handled, weighed or shot by this site.
