A barebow setup is a riser, a pair of limbs, a string, a tab and arrows. A plunger is tuning equipment rather than a requirement: barebow permits one, and three of the nine beginner bows compared here do not publish a plunger thread at all. You can buy the bow as one box or as separate parts, and this site compares three routes.

**A complete beginner takedown bow is the cheapest way to start**, at $99.95 to $199.99 for something you can shoot the day it arrives. The catch is the limb fitting. Eight of the nine bows compared use their maker's own pattern, or state no fitting at all, so heavier limbs must come from that maker and the riser is one you replace rather than keep. One is ILF and does not have that problem, and it is still not the pick for a beginner, because the lightest limb its maker publishes is 30 lb. The [beginner takedown recurve comparison](/bows/takedown-recurve/) sets out which is which, and what replacement limbs cost where anyone publishes a price.

**A setup built from parts costs $389.95 to $869.95**, and it does not buy what most people expect. The [intermediate setup comparison](/bows/intermediate-setup/) found the extra money does not buy upgradeability: the cheapest complete ILF bow already takes any ILF limb from any maker, so on the thing that decides whether a first bow lasts, the two routes are equal. What the extra buys is the weights themselves and a riser chosen on its own merits. Barebow bans stabilisers, so weights bolted to the riser are how the bow is balanced, and no complete takedown bow here ships any, though six of the nine do publish a stabiliser bushing to bolt one into.

**For a child, the only question is whether the bow grows.** [Youth recurve bows](/bows/youth-recurve/) run from $49.99 to $149.98, and the split is between takedown bows that accept heavier limbs on the same riser and one-piece bows fixed at one draw weight forever. That page also carries the only bow on this site weighed here rather than copied from a maker.

A complete bow is not always the cheaper route, which is worth checking before you assume it. On the two cases this site has priced, buying the parts separately saved nothing or cost more: a Galaxy Sage riser and Sage limbs come to exactly the price of the assembled bow, and the cheapest ILF riser and limb pair here costs $60.03 more than a complete ILF bow.

If you already know you are building from parts, the [riser hub](/risers/) and [limb hub](/limbs/) compare current models on their published specifications. Start there with the riser and the limbs, since those two decide draw weight and mass weight, the numbers everything else is tuned around.

If a poundage or a division question is holding up the purchase, the [start here guide](/start-here/) and the [barebow rules guide](/guides/barebow-rules/) cover what World Archery and the NFAA actually require, rather than what a retailer's marketing copy implies. Most people buying a first bow are shooting in a garden and never enter anything, and none of those rules applies until you do.
