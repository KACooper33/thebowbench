A barebow setup is a riser, a pair of limbs, a string, a tab and arrows. A plunger is tuning equipment rather than a requirement: barebow permits one, and two of the eight beginner bows compared here do not publish a plunger thread at all. You can buy the bow as one box or as separate parts, and this site compares three routes.

**A complete beginner takedown bow is the cheapest way to start**, at $99.95 to $199.99 for something you can shoot the day it arrives. The catch is the limb fitting. Every one of the eight bows compared is bolt-down, meaning limbs bolt straight to the riser on a pattern that maker chose. Heavier limbs come from that maker, and the riser is one you replace rather than keep. None of them is ILF. The [beginner takedown recurve comparison](/bows/takedown-recurve/) sets out which is which, and what replacement limbs cost where anyone publishes a price.

**A setup built from parts costs $389.95 to $869.95**, and it does not buy what most people expect. The [intermediate setup comparison](/bows/intermediate-setup/) prices what the extra buys: ILF, so limbs can come from any maker, the weights themselves, and a riser chosen on its own merits rather than bundled. Barebow bans stabilisers, so weights bolted to the riser are how the bow is balanced, and no complete takedown bow here ships any, though six of the eight do publish a stabiliser bushing to bolt one into.

**For a child, the only question is whether the bow grows.** [Youth recurve bows](/bows/youth-recurve/) run from $49.99 to $149.98, and the split is between takedown bows that accept heavier limbs on the same riser and one-piece bows fixed at one draw weight forever. That page also carries the only bow on this site weighed here rather than copied from a maker.

A complete bow is not always the cheaper route, which is worth checking before you assume it. A Galaxy Sage riser and Sage limbs bought separately come to exactly the price of the assembled bow, so taking it apart on the shelf saves nothing.

If you already know you are building from parts, the [riser hub](/risers/) and [limb hub](/limbs/) compare current models on their published specifications. Start there with the riser and the limbs, since those two decide draw weight and mass weight, the numbers everything else is tuned around.

If a poundage or a division question is holding up the purchase, the [start here guide](/start-here/) and the [barebow rules guide](/guides/barebow-rules/) cover what World Archery and the NFAA actually require, rather than what a retailer's marketing copy implies. Most people buying a first bow are shooting in a garden and never enter anything, and none of those rules applies until you do.
