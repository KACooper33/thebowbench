## What is the difference between Olympic recurve and barebow?

Barebow is the recurve division with two things taken away. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3) states it plainly: "Equipment permitted for the Barebow division is the same as for the Recurve division, with the following stipulations: No sight is allowed. No stabilisers are allowed."

That single sentence is why the switch is cheaper than most archers expect. The bow itself does not change. Both divisions shoot a riser with two flexible limbs and a single string, drawn and released with the fingers.

What changes is everything bolted to that bow, plus two measurements and one ring test. The rest of this page covers each in turn.

## What gear carries over from Olympic recurve to barebow?

Most of the expensive parts carry over. A recurve archer moving to barebow keeps the riser, the limbs, the arrows and usually the tab.

**Riser and limbs.** Both divisions use the same definition of a bow. There is no material restriction in either. The riser you already own is legal in barebow once the sight and stabilisers come off, provided the whole bow passes the ring test.

**Arrows.** The arrow rules are shared. [Article 9.1.7](#source-wa-book-3) sets a maximum shaft diameter of 9.3 mm and a maximum point diameter of 9.4 mm, and barebow inherits them. Your recurve arrows are legal barebow arrows.

**Accessories.** [Article 9.4.1](#source-wa-book-3) lists arm guards, chest protectors, bow slings, finger slings and quivers as permitted for all divisions. All of these carry over.

## What must you remove to shoot barebow?

Four items come off the bow, and each is banned by a named article.

- **The sight.** [Article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3): "No sight is allowed." Recurve permits one under article 9.1.5.
- **The stabilisers.** [Article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3): "No stabilisers are allowed." Recurve permits them under article 9.1.6.
- **The clicker.** [Article 9.3.4](#source-wa-book-3): "No draw check device may be used." Recurve permits one draw check indicator under article 9.1.4.
- **The nose or lip mark on the string.** [Article 9.3.2](#source-wa-book-3) permits no attachments to the string at all.

The bow must also be "free from protrusions, sights or sight marks, marks or blemishes or laminated pieces" inside the bow window that could help aiming, under [article 9.3.1](#source-wa-book-3). Any marking in the aiming area "must be taped over".

## Why does the plunger need moving?

The plunger stays, but it usually has to move back. This is the difference most recurve archers miss.

[Article 9.1.3](#source-wa-book-3) allows a recurve pressure point to sit "no further back than 4 cm towards the athlete from the pivot point of the grip". [Article 9.3.3](#source-wa-book-3) halves that for barebow: "no further back than 2 cm towards the athlete from the pivot point of the grip".

So a plunger set up legally for Olympic recurve can be illegal in barebow without anything being changed on it. Measure from the pivot point of the grip before an event rather than assuming the position carries over.

## Can you keep your recurve tab?

Usually yes. Both divisions permit an anchor plate, which is the feature most recurve tabs have and most other divisions ban.

[Article 9.1.8](#source-wa-book-3) permits recurve finger protection to incorporate an anchor plate, thumb or finger rests, finger straps, a finger separator, tab plates and plate extensions. [Article 9.3.7](#source-wa-book-3) permits barebow finger protection to include a separator and "an anchor plate or similar device attached to the finger protection (tab) for the purpose of anchoring".

Barebow adds one permission the recurve rules do not discuss, and one restriction. Marks may be added to the tab face, but they "must be uniform in size, shape and colour and may have up to two different lengths", and the stitching "must be uniform in size and colour". Additional memoranda are not permitted.

If you intend to string walk, a tab with crawl marks is the one piece of equipment worth buying new.

## Can you keep the nose or lip mark on your string?

No. This is the one string difference between the two divisions, and it is absolute.

[Article 9.1.2](#source-wa-book-3) permits a recurve bowstring to carry "one attachment ... to serve as a lip or nose mark". [Article 9.3.2](#source-wa-book-3) permits barebow no such thing: "No lip or nose marks or other attachments to the string are permitted."

A kisser button is an attachment to the string. It must come off before a barebow event.

## Are vibration dampeners allowed in both divisions?

Yes, dampeners are permitted in both, even though stabilisers are permitted in only one.

[Article 9.1.6](#source-wa-book-3) covers recurve: "Stabilisers and vibration dampeners are permitted to be attached to the bow." [Article 9.3.6](#source-wa-book-3) covers barebow, where the bow "including any combination of weights, vibration dampeners, and other attachments" must pass the 12.2 cm ring.

The [2022 World Archery bylaw](#source-wa-dampener-bylaw) is what made aftermarket dampeners explicitly legal in barebow, attached "directly to the riser or to weight(s)".

Barebow also gains something recurve does not need. Weights may be added below and above the grip, which is how a barebow archer replaces the balance a stabiliser used to provide.

## Can you use a wind indicator in barebow?

No. This is a small rule that catches archers moving across from recurve.

[Article 9.4.1](#source-wa-book-3) permits "wind indicators, non-electric or non-electronic ... attached to the equipment (e.g., light ribbons)", then restricts that line to the "(Recurve and Compound Divisions only)".

Every other accessory in article 9.4.1 applies to all divisions. The wind indicator is the exception.

## What does the 12.2 cm ring change?

The ring test applies to barebow and not to recurve, and it is what limits how much weight a barebow archer can add.

[Article 9.3.6](#source-wa-book-3) states that the bow, "braced or unbraced and regardless of the bow's orientation or angle", including any combination of weights, vibration dampeners and other attachments, "must pass through a ring with a diameter of 12.2 cm (+/- 0.5 mm) without moving, flexing or turning the attachments."

Two details decide setups. The test applies whether or not the bow is strung, and nothing may be moved, flexed or turned to get it through. A weight that has to be unscrewed to pass is not legal.

## How do the two divisions compare, item by item?

Every entry below is World Archery Book 3, version 2026-03-13.

| Item | Olympic recurve, article 9.1 | Barebow, article 9.3 |
|---|---|---|
| Sight | Permitted | Not allowed |
| Stabilisers | Permitted | Not allowed |
| Draw check or clicker | One indicator permitted | Not permitted |
| Vibration dampeners | Permitted | Permitted |
| Weights above and below the grip | Not needed, stabilisers used | Permitted |
| Plunger setback from the pivot point | 4 cm | 2 cm |
| Lip or nose mark on the string | One attachment permitted | Not permitted |
| Anchor plate on the tab | Permitted | Permitted |
| Marks on the tab face | Not restricted | Uniform, up to two lengths |
| String walking | Not applicable | Permitted |
| Wind indicator | Permitted | Not permitted |
| 12.2 cm ring test | Does not apply | Applies |
| Arrow shaft diameter | 9.3 mm maximum | 9.3 mm maximum |

## Should you switch from Olympic recurve to barebow?

The equipment cost of switching is low, because the riser, limbs and arrows carry over. What you remove is worth more than what you add.

Budget for three things. A stringwalking tab if you intend to string walk. A set of riser weights to replace the balance the stabilisers gave you. Possibly a plunger reposition, or a shorter plunger, to meet the 2 cm limit.

What changes most is not the gear. It is aiming without a sight and a clicker, which is a longer project than any purchase on this page.
