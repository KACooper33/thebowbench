## Is a cushion plunger legal in barebow?

A cushion plunger is legal in barebow. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.3](#source-wa-book-3) permits "an adjustable pressure button, pressure point or arrow plate ... provided they do not offer any additional aid in aiming". The [NFAA](#source-nfaa-styles) permits "an arrow rest and cushion plunger" in its Barebow Recurve style.

A cushion plunger is a tuning device, not an aiming aid. That is why barebow allows it while banning sights.

Two limits apply to the cushion plunger. World Archery article 9.3.3 states the pressure point "may be placed no further back than 2 cm towards the athlete from the pivot point of the grip". The NFAA adds a height limit: "No part of the arrow rest, cushion plunger or arrow plate may extend more than ¼ inch above the arrow in the sight window."

## Are stabilisers legal in barebow?

Stabilisers are not legal in barebow. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3](#source-wa-book-3) states it in four words: "No stabilisers are allowed."

This is one of only two blanket stipulations that article 9.3 adds to the recurve equipment rules. The other is "No sight is allowed."

## Are vibration dampeners legal in barebow?

Vibration dampeners are legal in barebow. They have been legal since 15 January 2022, when a [World Archery bylaw](#source-wa-dampener-bylaw) changed the rule.

The bylaw struck out the old wording, "Vibration dampeners fitted as part of the bow are permitted provided that they do not have stabilisers." It replaced that with a permission to install dampeners in the riser at manufacture, or to attach aftermarket dampeners "directly to the riser or to weight(s)".

The current wording sits in World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.6. The bow, "including any combination of weights, vibration dampeners, and other attachments, must pass through a ring with a diameter of 12.2 cm (+/- 0.5 mm) without moving, flexing or turning the attachments."

The NFAA agrees for Barebow Recurve: "Weights and / or vibration dampeners are permitted."

Limb dampeners are also permitted. They appear in the World Archery list of accessories allowed for all divisions.

## What is the difference between a stabiliser and a vibration dampener?

A stabiliser is banned in barebow and a vibration dampener is permitted, so the difference decides what you can buy.

A stabiliser extends from the riser on a rod and changes how the bow balances and holds. A vibration dampener absorbs vibration and mounts directly to the riser or to a weight. [World Archery article 9.3.6](#source-wa-book-3) requires that any attachment "must not aid the athlete in aiming or ranging in any way", and the whole bow must still pass the 12.2 cm ring.

Many gear lists treat stabilisers and dampeners as one banned category. That is wrong, and it costs barebow archers equipment they are allowed to use.

## Are string silencers legal in barebow?

The answer depends on which governing body runs the shoot. This is the one item on this page where the two rulebooks disagree outright.

The [NFAA](#source-nfaa-styles) permits string silencers in Barebow Recurve: "Brush buttons and string silencers, properly placed, may be used."

World Archery does not permit them in the barebow division. [Book 3 article 9.3.2](#source-wa-book-3) states: "No lip or nose marks or other attachments to the string are permitted." A string silencer is an attachment to the string.

Check the rulebook for the event you are shooting before you fit string silencers to a barebow setup.

## What is the 12.2 cm ring rule?

The 12.2 cm ring rule is the equipment test that decides whether a barebow setup is legal.

[World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.6](#source-wa-book-3) states that the bow, "braced or unbraced and regardless of the bow's orientation or angle", including any combination of weights, vibration dampeners and other attachments, "must pass through a ring with a diameter of 12.2 cm (+/- 0.5 mm) without moving, flexing or turning the attachments."

Two details in that wording change how you set a bow up. The test applies whether the bow is braced or unbraced, and nothing may be moved, flexed or turned to get it through.

The NFAA states the same diameter for Barebow Recurve: "The unstrung bow complete with permitted accessories shall be capable of passing through a hole or ring with a 12.2 cm inside diameter +/- 0.5 mm." The NFAA adds that the bow must fit "without having to flex vibration dampeners to fit through the ring".

## Where may weights be mounted on a barebow riser?

Weights may be mounted below and above the grip of a barebow riser. The [2022 World Archery bylaw](#source-wa-dampener-bylaw) states that "Weight(s) and dampener(s) may be added below and above the riser's grip, but must not aid the athlete in aiming or ranging in any way."

How they mount is restricted. Weights "shall mount directly to the riser without rods, extensions, angular mounting connections or shock-absorbing devices". A riser manufacturer's angled stabiliser insert is allowed, but angular brackets or connectors are not.

Whatever you fit, the complete bow must still pass the 12.2 cm ring.

## Is a clicker legal in barebow?

A clicker is not legal in barebow. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.4](#source-wa-book-3) states: "No draw check device may be used."

A clicker is a draw check device, so this rule bans it. The recurve division permits draw check indicators under article 9.1.4, which is why a clicker on a recurve bow is legal and the same clicker on a barebow setup is not.

## Is there a draw weight limit in barebow?

World Archery Book 3 sets no draw weight limit for the barebow division.

A 60 pound figure circulates widely for barebow. It comes from the compound division. [Book 3 article 9.2.1](#source-wa-book-3) states: "The peak draw weight must not exceed 60 lbs." That rule sits in the compound equipment article, not the barebow one.

Events, venues and age classes may set their own limits. Check the rules for the shoot you are entering rather than assuming a national figure.

## Is string walking legal in barebow?

String walking is legal in barebow. [World Archery Book 3 article 9.3.5](#source-wa-book-3) states: "Face and string walking are permitted."

Both aiming methods are allowed, which is why a stringwalking tab with crawl marks is standard barebow equipment. Article 9.3.7 permits marks on the tab, provided they "must be uniform in size, shape and colour and may have up to two different lengths".

## Where do World Archery and NFAA barebow rules differ?

| Item | World Archery barebow | NFAA Barebow Recurve |
|---|---|---|
| Cushion plunger | Permitted, article 9.3.3 | Permitted |
| Vibration dampeners | Permitted, article 9.3.6 | Permitted |
| Weights | Permitted, below and above the grip | Permitted |
| String silencers | Not permitted, article 9.3.2 | Permitted, properly placed |
| Ring test | 12.2 cm, +/- 0.5 mm, article 9.3.6 | 12.2 cm inside diameter, +/- 0.5 mm |
| Rest height above the arrow | Not stated in Book 3 chapter 9 | No more than ¼ inch in the sight window |

String silencers are the one outright disagreement. Everything else on this list matches.

## Which rulebook versions does this page quote?

Every rulebook quoted on this page is listed at the end, with its version and the day the link was last checked. The [World Archery rulebook index](#source-wa-rulebook-index) always carries the current version of every book.

Note the renumbering. The World Archery barebow equipment rules sat in Book 3 chapter 11 in earlier versions and are chapter 9 in the current one. Field and 3D rules are in Book 4. Older guides that cite article 11.4 are quoting a superseded numbering.

Rules change. Check the current rulebook before an event.
