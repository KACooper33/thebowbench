## Is there a draw weight limit in barebow?

No, and this is worth stating plainly because the internet says otherwise.

[World Archery Book 3](#source-wa-book-3) sets no draw weight limit for the barebow division. Article 9.3 covers barebow equipment and contains no poundage figure at all.

[USA Archery's own equipment specification](#source-usaa-achievement) sets none either. It defers the barebow definition to World Archery and states no maximum draw weight for the division.

Your club, your range, or a specific event may impose one. That is a local rule rather than a division rule, and the only reliable way to find it is to ask where you shoot.

## Where the 60 lb figure comes from

The 60 lb number is real. It is simply not a barebow rule.

[World Archery Book 3 article 9.2.1](#source-wa-book-3) states: "The peak draw weight must not exceed 60 lbs." That article sits in the compound equipment section. It has never applied to barebow.

This is the most repeated error in barebow gear writing, and it appears on the first page of search results.

## Where the 20 lb figure comes from

A 20 lb limit also circulates, usually attached to beginners or to young archers. It comes from one specific class in [USA Archery's Achievement Program](#source-usaa-achievement), and that class is not barebow.

The document defines Basic Compound as "a bow where the draw is mechanically varied by a system of pulleys or cams. Maximum draw weight will be 20 lbs." That is a class with a mechanical draw. A barebow archer is not shooting it, and the 20 lb ceiling does not carry across.

## Why governing body documents keep pointing at the wrong articles

Worth knowing if you go looking for the rules yourself.

USA Archery's equipment specification tells readers to find the recurve and compound definitions at "Book 3, Chapter 11.1" and "Chapter 11.2", and barebow at "Book 4, Chapter 22.3". Those chapter numbers are superseded. World Archery renumbered: recurve and compound are now Book 3 articles 9.1 and 9.2, and barebow is Book 3 article 9.3 for target archery and Book 4 article 19.3 for field.

Both World Archery links printed in that document return a 404, checked on 14 August 2026.

None of that makes USA Archery's own rules wrong. It does explain why so many gear sites quote article numbers that no longer exist: they are copying a national governing body's document that has not been updated. The [barebow rules guide](/guides/barebow-rules/) on this site quotes the current numbering throughout.

## Why this site will not tell you a poundage

There is no number here, and that is deliberate.

Draw weight is the one equipment decision on this site where a wrong answer injures somebody. Too much poundage too early causes shoulder and elbow injuries, and it builds form faults that take longer to remove than they took to learn.

The right poundage depends on your draw length, your build, how often you shoot, and what you are physically starting from. None of that is visible from a web page. A coach at a club watches you draw once and knows more than any article can tell you.

This site takes the same position on arrow spine, and for the same reason: publishing a formula would mean presenting a guess as this site's own finding.

## What actually changes the answer

**Draw length.** A longer draw pulls the limbs further and produces more weight at your fingers than the number marked on the limb. Limb poundage is measured at a standard draw length, not at yours.

**How often you shoot.** Poundage you can manage for six arrows is not poundage you can manage for a seventy-two arrow round. Sustainable is the target, not maximum.

**Your arrows.** Draw weight and arrow spine are coupled. Change poundage and your existing arrows may no longer match, so budget for a new set before changing limbs.

**Where you are in the sport.** Most archers move up in poundage over time. ILF limbs make that a limb purchase rather than a new bow, which is one of the practical arguments for an ILF riser.

## What the market actually offers

A useful reality check, because published limb ranges show what manufacturers expect beginners to buy.

The Galaxy Bronze Star, a wood-core entry limb, is published from **16 to 40 lb** in 2 lb steps. The WNS Motive C5, a carbon-foam limb aimed a step above entry level, is published from **20 to 46 lb**. Both figures are sourced from [Lancaster Archery Supply](#source-lancaster-limbs).

Note where entry-level limbs start. The bottom of that range exists because beginners genuinely start there, including adults. Starting at 16 or 18 lb is normal, not a failure.

## What to do instead of reading a number here

Go to a club and ask to be assessed. Most clubs run beginner courses that put a bow in your hand at a poundage a coach picked for you, which is both the safest and the cheapest way to find out.

If you are buying for a young archer, this applies more strongly, not less. Ask the coach before buying limbs.
